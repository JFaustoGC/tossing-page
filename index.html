A Sawyer robot grasps rigid parts and tosses them into target bins beyond its reach. The video shows an earlier evaluation; the results reported below are from the current paper and thesis.

Abstract

Tossing extends the reach of a manipulator, but its outcome depends on both the selected grasp and the release conditions. We generate dense grasp labels automatically in simulation using a geometric criterion, train a network to infer grasps from RGB-D observations, and refine the policy through simulated tosses. The selected grasp is combined with a ballistic model and a trajectory planner to generate the tossing motion. Both training stages run in simulation, and the network parameters remain fixed during physical deployment. Evaluated on a Sawyer robot, the system achieves grasp success rates of 97.56% for seen parts and 96.15% for unseen parts, with complete task success rates of 85.12% and 73.56%, respectively.

Method

Geometric pretraining. Candidate grasps are labeled using the gripper's opening range and finger-clearance checks. A U-Net is trained on RGB-D observations from 1,000 simulated scenes, split into 850 training scenes and 150 validation scenes.

Refinement from toss outcomes. Simulated grasp-and-toss attempts provide a target hit or miss label for the executed grasp. These outcomes continue training the policy initialized by geometric supervision.

Physical deployment. The network evaluates 32 orientations and selects the highest-scoring grasp. Its parameters remain fixed on the physical robot. The tossing primitive uses a global release-speed correction calibrated on the robot and held fixed during evaluation.

Experimental setup

A seven-degree-of-freedom Sawyer with a pneumatic parallel-jaw gripper picks parts from the tabletop. An RGB-D camera observes the pickup area. Twelve target bins form a three-row, four-column array, with row distances of 1.56, 1.72 and 1.90 m from the robot base.

All eight parts are placed together in the pickup area and replenished after the table is cleared. Targets are visited in randomly ordered cycles until 100 tosses have been executed for each part. Failed grasp attempts remain in the record, giving 826 attempts and 800 executed tosses in total. Physical outcomes are verified manually.

Grasp and complete task success are measured per attempt; tossing success is measured per executed toss. A successful toss enters the assigned bin, including cases followed by a bounce out. A toss that first enters a different bin is a miss.

Of the 171 physical task failures, 145 occur after acquisition. The evaluation is limited to the eight rectilinear shapes shown above, one physical instance per shape, and a prescribed tossing motion. The unseen group contains four shapes held out from training.

Grasp policy analysis

Across 256 recorded physical observations, the mean fraction of high-scoring pixels within the selected-part mask decreases from 39.35% after geometric pretraining to 6.92% after refinement. The refined network produces a smaller fraction in all 256 observations.
